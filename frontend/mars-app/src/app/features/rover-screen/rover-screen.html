Curiosity

NASA's Curiosity Rover

Curiosity is a car-sized, nuclear-powered robotic rover that landed in Mars' Gale Crater on August 6, 2012. Its main mission is to determine if Mars ever had environmental conditions suitable for microbial life.

Key Highlights

  • Major Discoveries: Curiosity confirmed its landing site was once an ancient freshwater lake bed containing the essential chemical ingredients for life. It has also detected complex organic molecules and seasonal methane fluctuations.
  • Mobile Geology Lab: The rover is equipped with 17 cameras, a rock drill, and a high-powered laser (ChemCam) that can vaporize rock layers from 7 meters away to analyze their composition.
  • The Sky Crane: Because it weighs nearly a ton, NASA used an innovative rocket-powered "Sky Crane" to lower the rover safely to the surface.

Current Status: Although its primary mission was only supposed to last two years, Curiosity remains operational after more than 13 years on Mars. It is currently climbing Mount Sharp to study how the planet transitioned from a wet world into a frozen desert.
